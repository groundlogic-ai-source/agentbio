# Prospective flagship selection rubric

This is a production screening instrument, not a benchmark and not a claim of
clinical validity. No metered case run should start until an exact
drug–disease-subtype–intended-use proposal passes every blocking item.

## Prespecified review

| Domain | Pass condition | Blocking condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disease identity | Exact Orphanet/Open Targets subtype resolves without umbrella substitution | Umbrella or mixed-mechanism disease concept |
| Causal target | Direct genetic or disease-mechanism anchor with visible provenance | Only pathway or naming proximity |
| Mechanism granularity | Drug action addresses the disease mechanism at the needed functional level | Coarse inhibitor/activator logic masks gating, trafficking, complex, or compartment biology |
| Literature limitation | Healthy bounded search; no confirmed applicable limitation | Guideline/systematic review or two independent records explicitly limit the exact class/use |
| Treatment landscape | Existing therapies and investigational approaches are enumerated | Candidate merely rediscovers an approved/current disease therapy without a distinct hypothesis |
| Candidate differentiation | Evidence distinguishes the candidate from same-class alternatives | Class effect is presented as a uniquely preferred molecule |
| Human pharmacology | Qualified human target evidence with provider-correct identifiers | Support is off-target, nonhuman only, or a safety-screen artifact |
| Disease specificity | Mutation/model/clinical evidence is labeled observed or unknown | Target pharmacology is restated as mutation or disease-model evidence |
| Exposure and route | Plausibility is supported or explicitly reviewable | Required tissue/cell/compartment exposure is known to be implausible |
| Safety | No confirmed withdrawal or use-specific disqualifying signal | Existing safety gate or use-specific limitation blocks prioritization |
| Source coverage | Required sources succeeded and empty results are genuine | Outage, degraded empty response, or unverified citation |

## Niemann–Pick disease type C stress-test assessment

The generic term “Niemann–Pick disease” fails the disease-identity rule because
types A/B and C have different causal biology. Only an exact NPC subtype and
genotype-qualified proposal is reviewable.

Niemann–Pick disease type C is suitable as a literature-gate stress test, but
is not presumed to be AgentBio's next-best flagship:

- Orphanet treats NPC as a specific entity (ORPHA:646).
- NPC1 and NPC2 genotypes can have different biology and response; mutation- and
  species-specific response is documented in the research literature.
- The treatment landscape is mature and recently changed: FDA approved
  arimoclomol in combination with miglustat and levacetylleucine in September
  2024.
- Miglustat and hydroxypropyl-beta-cyclodextrin have substantial established
  treatment/repurposing histories.

Therefore an NPC proposal advances only if it names the exact subtype/genotype,
candidate, mechanism, and intended use; survives the literature gate; and
demonstrates a distinct hypothesis rather than rediscovering current therapy.

## Selection record

For each shortlisted proposal, record:

1. exact disease/subtype and causal target;
2. candidate, class, action, and intended use;
3. literature-gate verdict and citations;
4. treatment-landscape overlap;
5. observed versus unknown mutation/model/clinical evidence;
6. exposure and safety constraints;
7. pass/reject reason against this rubric.

Only a passing record may authorize one metered flagship run.
