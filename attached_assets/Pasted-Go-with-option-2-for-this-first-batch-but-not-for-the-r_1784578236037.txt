Go with option 2 for this first batch, but not for the reason you might expect — this isn't really about cost, it's about being able to tell what's actually working.
Here's the methodological issue with running everything at once: if you enrich with both the free APIs and Boltz simultaneously, and the resulting hypotheses look meaningfully better, you won't know whether that's because of molecular descriptors and bioactivity data, or because of Boltz's binding-pose predictions specifically. You'd have spent money without learning which lever actually matters. Isolate the variable first.
There's also a real complexity reason to defer Boltz specifically, not just a sequencing preference: Boltz needs a resolved target to predict a binding pose against, and for a lot of repoDB's ~1,500 drugs, pinning down "the one target" cleanly (versus multi-mechanism drugs, combination therapies, or drugs with no single dominant target in ChEMBL) is genuinely fiddly — exactly the kind of scope creep that's turned "quick first test" into a multi-session debugging cycle before, several times in this project. The five free APIs don't have that problem; they're direct, factual lookups on drug identity alone.
So: free APIs only, run it live now — there's no reason to hold off on this part (option 3's caution), since ChEMBL/PubChem/BioGRID/Reactome/Open Targets are zero-cost factual lookups, and running it live is the only way to actually answer the real open question — does real chemical/biological enrichment break the name-matching rut at all. If it does, that's a strong signal Boltz is worth adding next as a targeted refinement. If it doesn't, that's useful information too, and it means the bottleneck isn't "missing features" and Boltz probably wouldn't have fixed it either — worth knowing before spending anything on it.
